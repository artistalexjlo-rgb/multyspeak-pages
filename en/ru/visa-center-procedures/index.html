Visa Center Applications and Collection: how it works in Russia — first-hand

Short answer
Schengen visa processing from Russia typically takes 4-7 weeks, with consulates occasionally granting longer durations than requested. Appointments are time-specific and non-transferable, so applicants often use bot services to secure slots or request combined dates via email for family members.

From real experience

An applicant in Moscow (Russia) submitted a Greece visa application on May 29th.
The EES status updated on June 25th, and the passport was received on July 1st. The visa granted was single-entry for 30 days, matching the invitation duration, despite the applicant only requesting 13 days. All standard documents were submitted (except SFR), including purchased tickets. The applicant had a strong visa history: two correctly used 6-month Italian multiple-entry visas within the previous 3 years.

To secure a Finnish visa application slot in Moscow, be aware that slots are released monthly between the 20th and 24th for the following month.
These slots are taken very quickly. Excessive activity on the booking system may lead to a temporary ban. An alternative approach for specific cases, such as visiting close relatives, could be to directly contact the Finnish Embassy to inquire about appointment availability, though this method's current feasibility may vary.

Consulate appointments are strictly time-bound.
Do not assume you can accompany a spouse on a different date; it is likely to be rejected. Recommended strategy: contact the consulate via email in advance to request a combined appointment date citing travel distance. Always ensure the visa subcategory (e.g., 'Other and tourism') matches the purpose of stay exactly; mismatches result in immediate rejection.

To secure an earlier visa application slot at VFS centers (e.g., in Moscow for Schengen visas) than those offered, users often resort to paid 'bots.' These bots monitor for 'cancellation slots' (slots freed up by other applicants' cancellations) and book them instantly.
It's noted that even these bot services can have long queues due to high demand.

Passport collection from a Visa Application Center usually requires the applicant's presence or a strictly defined power of attorney (notarized).
Even with a power of attorney, some centers demand the original submission receipt ('white slip'). Losing this receipt requires filing a loss statement, which is not always accepted for third-party pickups.

If you apply for a visa in St. Petersburg, Russia, and wish to receive your passport with the visa at a different city, you can arrange for courier delivery.
The specific courier services and their pricing should be inquired about directly at the visa application center (e.g., VFS Global), as these details can vary depending on the destination city.

To collect a spouse's passport from a Visa Application Center (e.g., VFS Global) in Russia, the collecting spouse must present their own Russian internal passport, the original receipts issued by the VFS during the document submission, and a marriage certificate.
It is also advisable to review the specific terms outlined in the service agreement from the VFS.

VFS Global SMS notifications can be unreliable or delayed.
If the online tracking status updates to 'Ready for Collection', contact the visa center directly if no SMS is received. Passports are generally available for collection at the VFS center shortly after the tracking status updates.
