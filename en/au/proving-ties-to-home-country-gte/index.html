GTE and Ties to Home Country in Australia: what actually matters to know

Short answer
The primary requirement for an Australian subclass 600 visa is demonstrating strong ties to your home country, such as stable employment, property, business interests, and dependent family members. A cover letter is essential to explain your intent to return, provide context for your financial situation, and address potential ambiguities in your documentation.

From real experience

A family from Kazakhstan applied for Australian tourist visas (subclass 600) to visit their son studying in Melbourne.
The husband, as a sponsor, provided proof of significant financial assets (over 200,000 AUD), real estate, and an existing business. They have another minor child remaining in Kazakhstan. The husband was granted a 1-year multi-entry visa (max 3 months stay) in 7 days. The wife was refused on day 6 (before biometrics) due to the visa officer's doubt about her intention to return to her home country, despite a strong travel history. This case suggests that even substantial financial proof can be misinterpreted as an intent to migrate, leading to refusal for one applicant while the other (sponsor) is approved. Key advice for re-application: strongly emphasize ties to the home country for the refused applicant, including family dependents (minor child staying home), business involvement, long-term commitments, and elderly parents. Ensure precise and consistent information, and review applications filled by third parties thoroughly. Apostille of documents is not required for Australian visa applications.

An Australian visa cover letter should primarily focus on strong ties to the applicant's home country and the genuine purpose of the visit, rather than simply listing past travels (which are visible in visa scans; visas from US, UK, Canada, NZ are generally more impactful than Schengen).
Key elements to include: details about family remaining in the home country (e.g., elderly parents, minor children, pets), stable employment, property ownership, or business interests. Use the letter to clarify any ambiguities in submitted documents (e.g., absence of leave dates in an employment certificate, reason for choosing budget accommodation). The letter serves to assure the visa officer of the applicant's intent to return.

For an Australian tourist visa (subclass 600), the applicant's travel history (or 'empty passport') is less critical than demonstrating strong ties to their home country.
Immigration officers prioritize evidence that the applicant intends to return. Key factors include stable employment (which serves as a significant tie), verifiable financial solvency, and family connections. If officially unemployed or with unclear sources of income, the applicant must provide a clear and comprehensive explanation of their financial situation and demonstrate other compelling reasons for their return. Transparency and strong evidence of ties to the home country are paramount to a successful application.

For Australian visitor visa applications, remote work is not an automatic ground for refusal if strong ties to the home country and an intent to return can be demonstrated.
It is crucial to provide a comprehensive employment certificate from your employer stating your position, salary, start date, and, importantly, approximate dates of leave/vacation and confirmation that you are expected to return to work. This documentation aims to reassure immigration officials of your non-immigrant intent, regardless of the remote nature of your work.

For Australian tourist visas, formal marriage is not always a strict requirement for sponsorship or demonstrating ties.
An unmarried partner remaining in the home country can act as a tie. Sponsorship documentation from a non-relative partner, including a sponsorship letter, employment certificate, and bank statements, can strengthen the application. While official documentation of the relationship may be limited, a cover letter and any available joint photos can be included.

An Australian visa application was refused because the stated 'anchor' (full-time study in Russia) was deemed insufficient.
The applicant's active travel history in 2024/2025 and being on academic leave during the application period led the visa officer to conclude that the study did not demonstrate strong enough ties to the home country. This highlights the importance of providing robust and current evidence of ties to the country of residence.

Visa refusals for subclass 600 often cite clause 600.211 (lack of genuine temporary stay intent).
Key factors leading to refusal include a lack of onshore contacts and insufficient proof of prior visa compliance in Australia or countries with similar immigration legislation.

A refusal for an Australian visa, even due to concerns about ties to an employer or lack of close relatives in the home country, does not necessarily preclude obtaining future visas.
It is advisable to carefully document ties to your home country and employer in subsequent applications.
✓ 1 report from chats
It is possible to obtain an Australian visa even with a passport that lacks previous visas from countries like the USA, UK, or Schengen Area.
The key factors considered are your ties to your home country, including stable employment, property ownership, and family relationships.
