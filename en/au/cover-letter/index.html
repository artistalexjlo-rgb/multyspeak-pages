Cover Letter in Australia: what actually matters to know

Short answer
Strengthen your Australian visa application with a concise cover letter emphasizing strong ties to your home country, such as employment and property. Provide 3-4 months of bank statements showing consistent income, use color scans for all uploads, and include English translations for documents, which can be self-translated without notarization.

From real experience

For Australian tourist visas, formal marriage is not always a strict requirement for sponsorship or demonstrating ties.
An unmarried partner remaining in the home country can act as a tie. Sponsorship documentation from a non-relative partner, including a sponsorship letter, employment certificate, and bank statements, can strengthen the application. While official documentation of the relationship may be limited, a cover letter and any available joint photos can be included.

A cover letter for an Australian visa application should primarily explain the reasons for returning to the applicant's home country.
It can also be used to clarify specific details or complexities in the application, such as having multiple bank accounts or explaining discrepancies. While not mandatory, it's a valuable tool to strengthen the application by demonstrating strong ties to the home country.

All supporting documents for an Australian visa must be translated into English.
For standard tourist visas, notarized or professional translations are not mandatory; self-translation, even using AI tools like ChatGPT, Google Translate, Claude, or Grok, is acceptable. It is not strictly necessary to add a 'Translated by me using AI' footer with contact details if you are the applicant. A general statement in the cover letter about using online translation services is sufficient. If a bank statement is issued in English but contains a Russian-language bank seal, the seal should ideally be translated. While some applicants have succeeded by only submitting translations, it's generally safer to include both the original document and its English translation.

When applying for an Australian visa and traveling with a partner who is a German citizen (eligible for ETA, which costs 20 AUD and is processed quickly, unlike the free eVisitor), it is advisable to attach a copy of the German passport to the visa application and include it in the cover letter as additional proof of joint travel.
For uploading passport documents: in the 'Travel documents' section, generally only the front page of the primary passport is required; empty pages are not needed. If an applicant has a second valid passport, some recommend uploading it with other old passports/visas, while others consider it unnecessary for the main 'Travel documents' category.

When applying for an Australian tourist visa (subclass 600) as a de facto couple, honesty and clear documentation are key.
While not strictly required for this visa type, providing evidence of your de facto relationship, such as joint travel history (photos, passport stamps) and a joint cover letter, can be beneficial. Focus on demonstrating your ties to your home country through employment, property (even rental agreements if applicable), and a well-articulated travel plan. Emphasize your intention to return home, which is a crucial factor for visa approval.

For Australian visa applications, if you have a lost passport with previous Schengen visas, you can mention this in your cover letter and provide supporting evidence if available, such as photos from Europe.
However, relying solely on old Schengen visas from a lost passport might not be a strong factor in your application, as visa officers may not be convinced by them. It's best to focus on current financial stability and the purpose of your trip to Australia. The application form has a section for 'Evidence of previous travels,' where you might be able to upload relevant documents or notes.

Australia Visitor Visa (subclass 600) documentation requirements: 1. National ID field does not require Russian internal passport data.
2. Cover letter should be concise (max 1 page) emphasizing strong ties to the home country (family, employment, property, history of timely departures). 3. Financial history: Avoid large sudden deposits in bank statements; 3-month history showing regular income is preferred. 4. Documents: Use color scans for all uploads. Certified translations are not required for tourist visa applications.

If a German citizen is accompanying a Russian applicant on an Australian visa but is not applying for a visa themselves (assuming they have visa-free entry or a different visa), providing a copy of their German passport might be considered supplementary information.
Mentioning their accompanying status in the cover letter is good practice. It is generally not mandatory to attach the passport of a visa-exempt accompanying traveler unless specifically requested.

Previous immigration and travel history is considered in Australian visa applications.
However, the weight given to this factor can vary, and outcomes are not always predictable. While extensive travel history can be beneficial, its absence does not guarantee refusal, and strong travel records do not ensure approval. Applicants should provide all required documentation and may consider a cover letter to explain their intentions.

When applying for an Australian visa with a sponsor but also contributing to the expenses, it's advisable to provide evidence of both your sponsor's financial capacity and your own.
Submit statements for both your main account (showing recent activity) and your savings account (showing accumulated funds). Clearly state in your cover letter that the trip is partially sponsored. The visa officers will review all provided financial documents.

When demonstrating financial capacity for an Australian visa, focus on providing a comprehensive bank statement (3-4 months) showing consistent income and reasonable spending.
Funds in a savings account are acceptable. Be prepared to explain the origin of significant recent deposits. The cover letter should emphasize your ties to your home country and reasons for return, rather than solely focusing on financial assets.

Internal passports and cover letters are generally not required documents for Australian visa applications unless specifically requested.
Regarding the passport, if Condition 8501 (requiring health insurance) is not stipulated in your visa grant, travel insurance is optional. Russian entry/exit stamps typically do not require translation as they are standard and understandable.

For visa applications, if a bank deposit was recently moved, provide a current balance certificate.
Include a brief explanation in the Cover Letter detailing the account nature (savings). For document translations (EGRN, employment certificates), standard procedure involves using browser-based translation tools and appending a personal signature with name and date to the resulting PDF.

For Australian visa applications, provide a bank statement showing the account balance and transaction history for the last three months.
The inclusion of a Russian internal passport number on bank statements is generally not required by Australian immigration. Consider writing a letter in English explaining your ties to your home country and reasons for visiting Australia.

When applying for an Australian visa, if you have prior military service, it is advisable to include this information in your cover letter and translated military ID.
Briefly describe your role and responsibilities, emphasizing any non-combat medical duties. This provides a more complete picture of your background.
