Using Wowpass and T-Money in South Korea: what actually matters to know

Short answer
The Wowpass card combines a prepaid debit card with integrated T-money functionality, which is essential for public transportation. You can top up both balances at dedicated kiosks or convenience stores, but T-money requires physical reloading or app-based transfers while the Wowpass balance can be loaded via currency exchange terminals.

From real experience

In South Korea, T-Money cards can be topped up with cash at any convenience store (CU, GS25).
Wowpass card machines for currency exchange and top-up are widely available, including 24/7 options in areas like Myeongdong (check the Wowpass app for locations). Exchange rates outside the airport, especially in popular tourist areas like Myeongdong, can often be more favorable than airport rates. Wowpass cannot be topped up online; only physical top-up at machines is possible. For Union Pay cards (e.g., RSKHB), it's possible to withdraw cash from ATMs (like Woori Bank) and then use the cash to load the Wowpass.

The T-money card is indispensable for using public transportation in South Korea, including subways, buses, and some taxis.
Purchase and top-up options are available at convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven), subway station vending machines, and other retail locations. The Wowpass card can also be loaded with T-money functionality. Usage involves tapping the card on designated readers upon entry and exit of subway stations or when boarding buses. Maintaining an adequate balance is crucial for seamless travel.

The Wowpass card can be obtained either in advance via the app or upon arrival at Incheon Airport.
Pre-ordering (at least 24 hours prior) is recommended for potential convenience, with options for pickup at airport CU stores or dedicated counters (if a SIM package is included). Alternatively, cards can be acquired from Wowpass machines located throughout the city. While airport exchange rates for topping up may be less favorable, cash exchange is still possible for initial travel needs.

A single Wowpass card is linked to one T-money account.
For multiple travelers, one Wowpass can be used, with additional T-money cards purchased separately for other family members. To obtain discounted fares for minors (under 18), a child's passport is required when applying for a youth tariff at a T-money top-up station. If seamless cashless payments are desired for everyone, consider that only one Wowpass can be integrated with a T-money account.

For using public transport in South Korea with children, separate transport cards (like Wowpass) are required for each child.
In buses, you may need to tap the card upon both entry and exit and inform the driver about traveling with a child, though purchasing individual cards is the safest approach. Metro travel also mandates a separate card per person.
