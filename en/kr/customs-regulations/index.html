Customs: Goods and Food Entry in South Korea: real experience from chats

Short answer
Travelers to South Korea should carry proof of funds, a travel itinerary, and return tickets, while ensuring K-ETA details match immigration statements. Meat and dairy products are strictly prohibited, and high-value tax-free items must be kept in carry-on luggage for customs inspection. For large quantities of goods like cosmetics, distribute items among multiple travelers to avoid the appearance of commercial resale.

From real experience

Travelers carrying medical equipment such as a garment steamer through airport security and customs should generally not face issues, provided it is for personal use and not in commercial quantities.
Unlike items with specific restrictions (e.g., liquids, certain electronics), personal garment steamers are typically allowed. However, it's always prudent to check the specific regulations of the airline and the destination country's customs authority to ensure compliance, especially if the item is of significant value or size.

When processing tax-free refunds at Korean airports, expect a detailed inspection for high-value items (around $1,000 USD each), such as phones, lenses, or jewelry.
These items must be kept in your carry-on luggage and presented to customs officials. For lower-value purchases like cosmetics, clothing, or souvenirs, inspections are generally less strict, and even multiple large luggage items are usually not an issue. The key is to ensure your total purchases do not appear to be a commercial quantity, which could raise questions.

Entry requirements for South Korea: 1. Carry cash ($100 per day) as proof of funds.
2. Have printed hotel reservations and return tickets. 3. Prepare a detailed daily travel itinerary. 4. K-ETA details (occupation, purpose of visit) must be consistent with statements given at the immigration desk. 5. Animal products (meat/dairy) are strictly prohibited; customs may record incidents, even if no immediate fine is issued.

Bringing cosmetics and clothing from South Korea to Russia for personal use typically does not raise concerns at customs, even with significant quantities.
Travelers have reported successfully bringing large amounts of cosmetics or multiple large suitcases (up to 23 kg each) without facing customs inquiries or issues. The assumption is that these items are for personal consumption rather than commercial resale.

When entering South Korea, be aware that food items, particularly meat products, are subject to strict customs regulations.
Travelers may have their food items confiscated at customs, especially if transiting through certain airports. It is advisable to check the official customs regulations of South Korea regarding food imports to avoid confiscation and potential penalties.

Importing cosmetics from South Korea to Russia/other countries: customs generally allow personal quantities.
Avoid commercial-looking shipments (e.g., dozens of identical items). If carrying large quantities, distribute items among several travelers to avoid triggering customs inspections for commercial intent.

When traveling to South Korea, it is generally not mandatory to bring prescriptions for common medications.
However, for controlled substances like neuroleptics and normothymics, it is highly recommended to carry a prescription, preferably translated into English. This can help clarify the purpose of the medication to customs officials if questioned.
