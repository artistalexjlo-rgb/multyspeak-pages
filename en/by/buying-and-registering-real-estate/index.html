Purchasing and Registering Property in Belarus: what actually matters to know

Short answer
To obtain a Temporary Residence Permit (VNZh) in Belarus, you must establish legal residency through property ownership or a long-term lease agreement. You must demonstrate financial solvency and ensure the property is habitable and meets specific living space requirements per person.

From real experience

For obtaining a Temporary Residence Permit (VNZh) in Belarus, establishing legal residency is key.
This can be achieved through a long-term lease agreement or property ownership. Rural properties in designated settlements are generally preferred over apartments for VNZh purposes, especially if they are habitable. It's important to note that registration in a property under construction might not be sufficient for VNZh application as it may not grant immediate residency rights.

Obtaining a Belarusian Residence Permit (VNZh) typically requires specific grounds such as permanent registration, employment, enrollment in an educational institution, or property ownership.
While owning property may facilitate the process, the specific requirements can be complex. Some individuals report difficulties and rejections even with valid grounds, sometimes being advised to marry a Belarusian citizen. Enrollment in an educational institution can serve as a basis for a Temporary Residence Permit (RVP), which can then potentially lead to VNZh after further steps like establishing a business (IP) or obtaining permanent registration.

Purchasing vacant properties in Belarus for a nominal fee (around one basic unit) is a potential pathway to obtaining residency (VNZh).
However, the property must be habitable and suitable for year-round living, not a ruin. The process of obtaining VNZh after purchase can still be complex, with potential rejections even for homeowners, sometimes suggesting marriage as an alternative. Resources like the registry of vacant houses ('eri2nca.by') can provide listings.

Purchasing a property in Belarus can be a basis for obtaining a Temporary Residence Permit (RVP).
However, the property must be habitable year-round. If purchasing a dilapidated house, significant investment in repairs will be required, and alternative accommodation might be needed during renovations. Unlike the permanent residence permit (VNZh), RVP offers more flexibility, allowing for renting an apartment while renovating or building.
