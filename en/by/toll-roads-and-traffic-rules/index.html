Toll Roads and Traffic Rules in Belarus: what actually matters to know

Short answer
Pay all traffic fines promptly, as unpaid violations can lead to a five-year entry ban. For vehicles under 3.5 tons with EAEU license plates, Belarusian highways are generally toll-free, while the speed limit tolerance is strictly 10 km/h.

From real experience

Foreigners driving in Belarus should be aware that unpaid traffic fines can lead to a 5-year entry ban.
Fines should be paid promptly. If a fine is issued, it can often be checked or paid at traffic police posts (GAI) or via local banking channels. Ignoring or failing to pay a small fine can result in serious legal consequences, including being officially informed of a ban by transport police during travel.

Belarus uses speed cameras, indicated by 'Фота Кантроль' signs.
Cameras are mainly on rural roads. Fines are not sent to Russian Gosuslugi and can only be checked with Belarusian GAI. Unpaid camera fines expire after three months unless stopped by police.

For cars with Russian license plates, road tolls in Belarus are generally free.
However, if you wish to pay for passage on toll roads like the MKAD (Minsk Ring Road), you can do so via the Autodor-tr.ru website. Local Belarusian insurance is recommended over the Russian 'blue card' for car repairs within Belarus during extended stays, as the blue card may require repairs to be done in Russia. The cost for local insurance is approximately 120 BYN for two months or around 2500 BYN monthly, with annual plans offering better value.

When traveling from Belarus to Russia with a non-owned trailer, ensure you have the trailer's documentation and a 'blue card' (international insurance).
For extended stays in Belarus, consider purchasing local Belarusian insurance, as the 'blue card' may not cover vehicle repairs within Belarus, potentially requiring you to transport the vehicle back to Russia for service. Russian-plated vehicles generally have free passage on Belarusian toll roads. 'Blue card' insurance allows for repairs in Russia if an accident occurs in Belarus, but not vice versa.

Fuel availability in Belarus is stable for passenger cars.
For transit from Moscow to Belarus, fill up before crossing the border. On the return route (M1 towards Moscow), fuel shortages are reported at some stations; limits of 30 liters per refueling may apply. Apps indicating real-time fuel availability are often unreliable. Road tolls for cars under 3.5 tons registered in EAEU countries are free on Belarus highways.

Traffic violations by foreign-registered vehicles in Belarus are processed with priority, and fine information is usually available within a few days.
Exceeding the speed limit significantly, such as by 60 km/h, will result in a substantial fine. Prompt payment is advised to avoid issues with exiting the country. Some travelers suggest using alternative routes to bypass potential border checks.

There is no fuel shortage in Belarus. Russia-Belarus transit on M-roads may experience severe fuel supply issues in the Pskov region (long queues).
Speed limit tolerance in Belarus is 10 km/h (unlike 20 km/h in Russia). Traffic fines from cameras are stored in the database; police can stop vehicles for unpaid fines at any time.

Cameras in Russia do capture speeding and other traffic violations for Belarusian vehicles.
While unregistered fines may theoretically be dismissed, it is advisable to comply with Russian traffic laws to avoid potential issues.

Toll sections on the Minsk highway (e.g., between km 33 and 66) can be paid for at toll booths using cash or card.
Transponders are not mandatory. Payment is collected upon passing the toll point. An alternative route near Odintsovo exists but may not be cost-effective.

Specific toll sections might exist, but standard EAEU registrations are usually exempt. Confirm specific road charges if any exist.
