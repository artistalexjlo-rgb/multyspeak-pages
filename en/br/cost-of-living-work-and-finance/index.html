Cost of Living, Work, and Finance in Brazil: real experience from chats

Short answer
Courier earnings typically range from BRL 3,000 for motorcycle riders to BRL 8,000 for car owners, while English teachers earn between R$ 25 and R$ 200 per hour. Due to relatively low local wages, financial stability often depends on securing remote employment or high-demand skilled work rather than relying on local labor.

From real experience

Courier earnings in Brazil can vary significantly based on location, delivery platform, mode of transport, and hours worked.
* **iFood Deliveries:** In cities like Vitória (Sudeste region), couriers using a car can earn between BRL 6,000 to BRL 8,000 per month, potentially up to BRL 10,000 if they own the vehicle and manage multiple trips daily. Deliveries in major cities like Rio de Janeiro or São Paulo might offer similar or potentially higher earnings due to higher demand, but also face more traffic congestion. * **Motorcycle/Bicycle:** Earnings for motorcycle or bicycle couriers are generally lower, typically ranging from BRL 3,000 to BRL 4,000 in metropolitan areas. * **Expenses:** It is crucial to factor in vehicle maintenance, fuel, insurance, and platform fees when calculating net income.

English teaching salaries in Brazil range from R$25-R$50 per hour in language schools and R$60-R$200 per hour for private tutors.

Perceptions of pricing for foreigners in Brazil vary.
While some report instances of 'gringo pricing,' where costs are significantly inflated (e.g., five times higher when perceived as non-local), others experience fair pricing even in tourist areas like Bahia. The perception is that while exceptions exist, fair pricing is more common than systematic overcharging, though tours for Americans are cited as an example of potentially high markups.
