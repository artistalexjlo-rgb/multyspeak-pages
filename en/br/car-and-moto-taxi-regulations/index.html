Car and Moto-Taxi Services: how it works in Brazil — first-hand

Short answer
Uber and 99 are the most reliable and cost-effective transport options in Brazil, offering fixed fares and digital trip records that prevent overcharging common in traditional street taxis. For safety, avoid displaying valuables in public, steer clear of desolate areas at night, and refrain from carrying regulated self-defense weapons.

From real experience

In Brazil, the experience with ride-sharing services like Uber is generally more reliable than in Egypt regarding direct price scams or persistent haggling.
Uber drivers typically do not attempt to overcharge, especially when paying by card. However, it is crucial to ensure the driver ends the trip in the app to avoid potential phantom debts. Conventional street taxis are riskier and prone to significantly overcharging foreigners. The primary safety concern in Brazil shifts from financial scams to personal property theft; tourists should be vigilant with belongings, avoid displaying phones openly, and steer clear of dark or deserted areas, especially at night.

In Brazil, the priority of pedestrians can vary significantly by city.
In smaller towns, drivers are generally more courteous and tend to yield to pedestrians, even when not strictly necessary. However, in larger cities, such as São Paulo, the situation can be different, with pedestrians sometimes having to wait longer at traffic lights. Motorists generally have a more comfortable experience due to amenities like climate control, whereas pedestrians may face challenges in being noticed, particularly at crosswalks.

Self-defense items like pepper spray are strictly regulated in Brazil and may lead to legal issues.
Avoid carrying defensive weapons. Basic safety protocol: avoid desolate areas, use Uber/99 for transport, keep expensive items hidden, and maintain high situational awareness.
