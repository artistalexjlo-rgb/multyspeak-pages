Residency, Citizenship, and Resident Documents in Brazil: what actually matters to know

Short answer
To work in Brazil, you must secure an employer sponsor for a VITEM V visa and register with the Federal Police upon arrival. Residency applications require apostilled and sworn-translated documents, and scheduling Federal Police appointments often requires using online booking tools due to limited availability.

From real experience

To obtain a work visa (VITEM V) in Brazil as a Russian citizen, the process is as follows: 1. Find a Brazilian employer willing to sponsor your employment.
2. The employer must apply for a work permit from the Ministry of Labor and Employment, justifying the need to hire a foreigner and adhering to local labor balance regulations. 3. Once the work permit is granted (typically 15-30 days), you can apply for the VITEM V visa through the Brazilian consulate, submitting required documents like passport, photos, criminal record check, and proof of qualifications. 4. The visa application processing can take 2-4 months. 5. Upon arrival in Brazil, you must register with the Federal Police within 30 days to obtain a foreigner ID card (CRNM). The entire process can take several months to half a year.

For foreigners relocating to Brazil, finding employment without proper Brazilian documents (RNM, CPF, work permit) and Portuguese language skills is extremely difficult.
English and basic Spanish are generally not sufficient for most jobs. Even with refugee status, the initial period requires obtaining temporary documents (paper RNM instantly, plastic RNM in months, CPF in days) and an electronic work permit. Job search platforms like local equivalents of 'hh' (HeadHunter) can be used, but opportunities are scarce without Portuguese. Sao Paulo generally offers higher salaries than cities like Fortaleza. Jobs requiring minimal verbal communication (e.g., sanitation, cleaning) are more accessible. Finding work that includes accommodation is highly unlikely.

To obtain a Brazilian passport for a child born in Russia who has acquired Brazilian citizenship by naturalization (e.g., through a sibling), the following documents are typically required: proof of Brazilian citizenship (Portaria de Naturalizacao), RNM (Registro Nacional Migratório), CPF (Cadastro de Pessoas Físicas), and the child's Russian birth certificate with an apostille and sworn translation.
While the Federal Police might request a CIN (Carteira de Identidade Estrangeiro), it is advisable to inquire directly with the Federal Police department about the specific documentation accepted for passport applications in such cases, as practices can vary.

The ENCCEJA exam (Ensino Médio) is a free government-run school equivalency exam in Brazil that can be used as proof of Portuguese language proficiency for citizenship, serving as an alternative to the harder CELPE-Bras exam.
Registration typically occurs in May, exams are held in August, and results are published in December. The exam consists of multiple-choice questions in various school subjects and an essay. If a candidate fails specific subjects, they only need to retake those specific subjects in subsequent years. Venue information is released in the candidate's portal approximately 10 days before the test.

When crossing the border from Argentina to Brazil by bus, ensure you understand the border control procedures.
While Argentina may have an electronic system, Brazil requires physical entry stamps for immigration purposes, especially if you intend to formalize local documents or residency. It is advisable to communicate your need for border stamps to the bus driver to ensure proper processing on the Brazilian side to avoid being considered an illegal entrant. The stamp is crucial for subsequent administrative processes in Brazil, such as applying for local residency permits.

In Brazil, access to police services generally requires an appointment.
Some cities allow for stay extensions without an appointment. In Recife, appointment slots are typically released within the current month, offering multiple openings over several days. Securing an appointment requires prompt action, often within 15 seconds of release. While not guaranteed, attempting to visit without an appointment might occasionally result in service.

The Brazilian Federal Police residency appointment process varies significantly by location and individual officer.
Requirements such as proof of school enrollment or language proficiency can be requested at the officer's discretion. There is no unified global experience; appointments for residency and naturalization often follow different protocols and office queues.

Scheduling appointments with the Federal Police in Brazil for residency (VNM) can be extremely difficult due to a lack of available slots.
Official channels, including the website, may offer no solution. Utilizing specialized bots that announce appointment openings or automate the booking process, or attempting appointments in different cities like Rio de Janeiro, may be necessary.

To get married and obtain residency in Brazil, all required documents must be translated by a specialized translator and apostilled.
Residency applications can be submitted after marriage, typically taking over three months. Documents with apostille remain valid during this process, even if the applicant doesn't leave the country.

Documents from Russian consulates abroad are generally insufficient for residency purposes in Brazil.
The correct procedure is to obtain original documents from Russia (like Birth Certificates), apostille them in Russia, and have them sent to Brazil via a courier or personal connection to be translated by a certified (juramentado) translator.

To apply for Brazilian residency, a police clearance certificate is mandatory.
If obtained in Russia, it requires an Apostille. The document must be translated into Portuguese. If the applicant is already in Brazil, the certificate can be requested at the Russian Consulate, a process that typically takes 5-7 business days.

To apply for a Brazilian passport for a minor after citizenship confirmation, the applicant must schedule an appointment with the Policia Federal.
Bring the protocol number provided during the citizenship confirmation process and all required identification documents to the scheduled Federal Police office.

Naturalization in Brazil language requirement: CELPE-Bras is the standard, 100% accepted document.
University Portuguese course certificates are accepted if the institution is licensed, but they must include an in-person exam. Online courses are generally rejected by the Federal Police.
