Safety and Situational Awareness in Brazil: real experience from chats

Short answer
Avoid displaying expensive electronics, jewelry, or large amounts of cash in public, as phone snatching and petty theft are common. Dress inconspicuously, use rideshare apps for transportation, and never leave your belongings unattended on the beach.

From real experience

Safety guidelines for Brazil: 1. Avoid displaying expensive phones or jewelry in public, especially in traffic.
2. Do not walk in deserted areas or near favelas at night. 3. Use taxi/rideshare apps for transportation. 4. Use the 'Fogo Cruzado' app to track real-time security alerts (shootings/clashes). 5. Beach security: do not leave belongings unattended; ask locals or families nearby to watch items if necessary.

In Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo, prioritize personal safety due to high crime rates.
Avoid displaying expensive items like jewelry, watches, or high-end phones, as they attract criminal attention. Dress simply, wear casual footwear like flip-flops, and carry minimal cash. Use a basic phone for essential communication and ride-hailing services. It's advisable to leave valuables and important documents in a hotel safe. Be aware of common theft methods, including phone snatching at public transport stops, crosswalks, and even in cafes. While popular tourist sites can be targets, some visitors report feeling safer in favelas when accompanied by a certified local guide. Always assess the specific area and exercise situational awareness.

For safety in Rio de Janeiro, tourists should avoid openly displaying valuables such as phones, expensive jewelry, or large amounts of cash.
