International Banking and Cards in Brazil: real experience from chats

Short answer
Carry new-design USD for cash reserves, as older bills or Russian rubles are difficult to exchange. Digital payments via Google Pay or Apple Pay are preferred over physical cards due to skimming risks, and cryptocurrency or P2P services like Pix-linked platforms often offer more favorable rates than traditional banks.

From real experience

When traveling to Brazil, dollars are generally preferred over euros for exchange.
Cards are widely accepted, even by street vendors, but cash might be needed for minor expenses (e.g., public toilets). For card payments, always say 'crédito'. Be aware of card skimming; using digital payment methods like Google Pay or Apple Pay is safer than physical cards. Some non-Brazilian cards (e.g., Kazakhstan's Freedom Card) may not work in all terminals (e.g., ~30% of 'orange' terminals) or require insertion instead of tap. Certain banks (e.g., Halyk) might require prior notification of travel to Brazil for card functionality. Avoid exchanging currency at airports due to unfavorable rates. When bringing USD cash, ensure bills are of the new design; old-style dollar bills may be rejected or exchanged at a significantly worse rate (e.g., a difference of up to 1.5 BRL per USD compared to new bills).

Cryptocurrency is presented as a more advantageous method for transferring funds from Russia to Brazil compared to traditional banking.
The process involves converting rubles into cryptocurrency (e.g., USDT) through official exchanges with a 1-2% commission or via P2P transactions (though P2P carries a risk of Russian bank card blocking). Subsequently, the cryptocurrency is converted into Brazilian Reals. This method is reported to result in minimal exchange rate losses, often up to 2%. Many Russians utilize platforms like Bybit for this purpose, paying via QR codes. This approach is generally considered faster and more cost-effective than bank transfers due to lower fees and better exchange rates. It is recommended to use cryptocurrency primarily as a transit method rather than for long-term storage.

When traveling to Brazil, it's recommended to have Brazilian Reais or US Dollars.
Russian 'Mir' cards are not accepted in Brazil, nor are Russian cards generally accepted in countries like Kazakhstan. While credit/debit cards are widely used, carrying some cash is advisable for smaller purchases or in places where cards might not be accepted. Exchanging currency at the airport often yields unfavorable rates; local exchange bureaus ('cambio') may offer better rates but operate primarily on weekdays. Uber is noted as a cost-effective option for transportation compared to other services, which can be as expensive as in Moscow or higher.

Exchanging Russian Rubles (RUB) for Brazilian Reais (BRL) in Brazil is extremely difficult.
Most standard currency exchange offices do not accept RUB. Digital services and P2P platforms (e.g., Moneytrans) that utilize the SBP (Russian System of Fast Payments) to Pix (Brazilian Instant Payment System) are the recommended method for transferring funds from Russia to Brazil.

The optimal currency exchange method (cash vs.
international card) in Brazil is not straightforward and can be contradictory. One user noted MasterCard rates around 5.12 BRL/USD, while exchange offices offered 5.5 BRL/USD (initially seeming better). However, another user reported unfavorable cash exchange rates at local offices (around 4.8 BRL/USD) and preferred using their foreign card. Websites like MelhorCambio are available for rate comparison. Travelers should check current rates upon arrival and consider that foreign card rates might sometimes be more favorable than cash exchanges in physical offices.
