Using Foreign Cards (CIS, Turkey, Thailand) in Brazil: what actually matters to know

From real experience

The functionality of Thai bank cards (e.g., Bangkok Bank) in Brazil can be inconsistent.
While some international cards may work for payments or ATM withdrawals, it's not guaranteed. Factors like the card's BIN (Bank Identification Number), network affiliation (Visa, Mastercard), and the specific terminal or ATM can affect acceptance. It's advisable to have alternative payment methods, such as local currency or other international cards known to work in Brazil.

For Russian citizens traveling to Brazil, payment strategies include: 1. Carrying a small amount of cash for immediate needs (like airport transfer) and keeping the rest secured.
2. Using cards from countries like Kazakhstan or Kyrgyzstan, which generally have better acceptance rates in Brazil for both card payments and ride-sharing apps like Uber. 3. Be aware that Belarusian or Tajik bank cards may have unreliable functionality and unfavorable exchange rates. 4. Setting up a virtual Brazilian bank account via cryptocurrency is complex for newcomers. A combination of a reliable foreign card and a small amount of cash is often recommended. Online payments may sometimes be flagged as suspicious.

Kyrgyzstan-issued bank cards, specifically mBank, are reported to work for payments in Brazil.
Payments via physical card or mobile payment systems like Google Pay are generally successful. However, online transactions with these cards may sometimes be declined as suspicious. When paying with foreign cards, some merchants or banks may apply a surcharge of 10-15% above the official exchange rate; this rate variability can make it difficult to predict the final charge. Crucially, when given the option, always choose to pay in Brazilian Reals (BRL). Paying in your card's native currency (e.g., USD) can result in an unfavorable exchange rate, often 15% worse than paying in BRL.

Certain foreign bank cards, such as Bakai Bank cards, are reported to function in Brazil.
When making payments at a point-of-sale terminal, always select to be charged in Brazilian Reals (BRL) to obtain a more favorable exchange rate compared to payment in a foreign currency. For hotel bookings via platforms like Booking.com, the available payment methods (online prepayment, on-site payment, cash) are dependent on the specific tariff chosen for the reservation.

Belarusian Mastercard (BNB Bank) can be used in Brazil, primarily via contactless payment, as chip and magnetic stripe functionality can be unreliable.
Brazil has widespread card acceptance, including contactless payment terminals. However, it is advisable to carry some cash and have multiple cards from different banks, as not all terminals may accept foreign cards. Russian cards may have limited acceptance.

Users have reported significant issues with Freedom Bank cards in Rio de Janeiro.
Problems include complete non-functionality, random negative balances that take days to resolve, and arbitrary card restrictions. Customer support response times are also reportedly slow, making it advisable for travelers to have alternative payment methods.

Russian bank cards are generally non-functional in Brazil.
UnionPay (RSCHB) works in select 'green' ATMs. Travelers are advised to use foreign bank cards for daily operations. Premium bank subscriptions in Russia are recommended for bundled travel insurance and airport lounge access.

Foreign debit cards issued in CIS countries often require selecting the 'crédito' option on Brazilian payment terminals to function, as the local payment system interprets them as credit cards.
Some terminals or ATMs may occasionally decline them, so having alternative payment methods is advised.

Turkish bank cards, specifically from Vakifbank and Isbank, may experience acceptance issues in Brazil, particularly on toll roads with proprietary payment systems like SoloDebito.
While some Turkish Visa cards work, others do not. Advise users to have alternative payment methods in Brazil.

The user specified credit cards, but confirmed they were functioning, implying they might have been debit cards in practice or that the distinction did not cause problems.
