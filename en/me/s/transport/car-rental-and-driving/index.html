Car Rental and Driving in Montenegro: what actually matters to know

From real experience

riskWhile Montenegrin law permits a small blood alcohol content (BAC) for drivers, tourists should exercise extreme caution.
The legal limit is low, and tourists may face stricter enforcement and significant fines compared to locals. New traffic regulations are expected to further tighten restrictions on drunk driving. It is advisable for tourists to avoid driving after consuming alcohol and opt for taxis or alternative transportation.

tipFor long-term car rentals (minimum one month) in Montenegro, it is recommended to research reputable rental agencies.
While major international brands may operate, local companies can sometimes offer better rates. It is advisable to check reviews for reliability and customer service. Consider factors like insurance coverage, mileage limits, and the condition of the vehicles. Booking in advance is recommended, especially during peak season.

ruleParking in Budva can be challenging, especially near residential areas or apartments without dedicated parking.
Options may include paid public parking lots or street parking, though availability can be limited. Some residents might offer private parking spaces for a fee. Inquire locally about available options or consider using public transport or walking if feasible.

ruleRussian registered vehicles entering Bosnia and Herzegovina from Montenegro require a separate 'border insurance' policy as Russia is no longer part of the Green Card system.
Local Montenegrin insurance (e.g., Lovcen) is not valid in BiH. Be aware of bank/office closures during local holidays. Insurance can often be obtained at border crossings (e.g., Klobuk) until 18:00 local time.

riskDriving Russian-plated vehicles into Montenegro via EU/Balkan borders (Bulgaria, Baltic states, Turkey) is currently highly restricted or impossible.
Authorities often deny entry or transit to vehicles with Russian license plates regardless of the driver's citizenship. It is recommended to sell such vehicles in Russia and purchase a car locally in Montenegro.

ruleWhile speed cameras are present in Montenegro, their operational status and enforcement mechanisms are in development.
Cameras are often positioned at city entry and exit points, primarily for traffic monitoring rather than immediate speed violation ticketing. The legal framework for speed enforcement is not yet fully established.

serviceFor urgent car maintenance such as a flat tire on a rental car without a spare, locate a 'Šlep Služba' (tow truck service) in the nearest town.
Contacting them is the primary method for obtaining assistance, which may involve tire repair or replacement. This service operates on a call-out basis.
